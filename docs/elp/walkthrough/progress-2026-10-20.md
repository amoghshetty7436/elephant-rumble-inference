Tuesday, 20 October 2026: results of the 8 training setups

•          The run of all 8 setups finished on the lab PC (4 h 13 min: 3 h embedding, about 9 min per setup). Full results, with charts and plain explanations: https://claude.ai/artifact/8E4os4fqgYxgJJb5W1foG6
•          Winner: setup E, clearing recordings + 254 noise clips (thunder, rain, wind, vehicles, etc.) as “not rumble” examples. Picked on the validation days, then confirmed on both tests.
–         Test 2 (Sample A), compared with FruitPunch’s mix (setup A): precision 0.72 → 0.81, recall 0.70 → 0.68, false alarms 0.17 → 0.10 per hour.
–         Test 1 (Sample B): precision 0.59 → 0.69, recall 0.85 → 0.83.
–         Vehicle days: false alarms 0.93 → 0.28 per hour.
•          Main findings:
–         Clearing data does not hurt forest performance. Forest only (B) was the worst setup (precision 0.20 on Test 1) because the forest set has 9 times fewer rumble examples. Clearing only and clearing + forest scored about the same.
–         Teaching the model what noise sounds like gave the biggest gain, even though the noise clips were only 7% of the “not rumble” examples.
–         Moving more forest days into training (F, G, H) did not help: they add only 4–8% more rumble examples, and F and H gave more false alarms.
–         FruitPunch’s balanced-clip test scores every setup 0.95–0.96, so only the whole-day test shows the real differences.
–         The validation days ranked the setups in the same order as both tests, so choosing on validation works.
•          Caveats: one swamp day (nn08c_20230104) holds 1,032 of Test 1’s 1,577 rumbles, and each setup was trained once, so gaps of about 0.02 may be chance.
•          Follow-up run: setup E with the forest days added back (clearing + forest + noise, “E2”).
–         Practice days: better than E (overall score 0.68 vs 0.65).
–         Tests: a trade, not a win. It finds more rumbles (recall 0.86 vs 0.83 on Test 1, 0.73 vs 0.68 on Test 2) but gives more false alarms (precision 0.65 vs 0.69 and 0.76 vs 0.81; vehicle days 0.79 vs 0.28 per hour). Overall scores are within 0.01 of E.
–         Decision: build on E2, because finding rumbles (recall) matters more than avoiding false alarms for an endangered species.
•          How models will be compared from now on: false alarms per hour at a fixed recall on the validation days (80% as the main target, also shown at 85% and 90%), averaged over 3 training runs. A literature check confirmed this is common practice for detectors where missed calls are costly (e.g. ELP’s own rumble detector, Keen et al. 2017, ran at 80–89% recall).
•          Next steps (one change at a time, judged on the validation days, tests scored only at the end):
–         Smarter alarm rules on the saved scores (smoothing, minimum length, a lower “on” level than 0.5, finer cut-offs), no retraining.
–         A bigger share of noise and other “not rumble” examples.
–         A longer listening window, from 0.64 s up to about 12 s in small steps, with inference time measured for each model.
–         Retraining on the detector’s own false alarms.
