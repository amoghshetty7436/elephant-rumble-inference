# Post-processing of frame-level probabilities in sound / bioacoustic event detection: thresholds, smoothing, minimum duration, hysteresis, and recall

Context: a FruitPunch-style rule (frames with p > 0.5 for >= 4 consecutive 0.64 s frames -> one detection, scored by mean probability) means the detection *set* is fixed by the 0.5 activation threshold and the 4-frame (~2.56 s) minimum duration. Sweeping a cut-off on the mean-probability score can only remove detections, so recall is capped at whatever the 0.5/4-frame rule produces. The literature below bears on whether that cap is necessary.

Bibliographic note: where a venue/DOI is given from my own knowledge rather than read on a page this session, it is marked "(venue/DOI not verified this session)".

## Q1. How DCASE SED (Task 4) and few-shot bioacoustic (Task 5) systems choose activation thresholds, median filters, minimum durations; tuned on validation? Does lowering the threshold / hysteresis raise recall?

### Takeaway
Baselines often ship with a fixed 0.5 threshold and a fixed median filter, but the literature consistently shows these post-processing parameters change scores a lot (e.g. MIL-system F1 15.3% to 43.9% depending on post-processing alone), that optimal values are class-dependent and operating-point-dependent, and that serious systems tune them (thresholds, per-class median windows, min-duration/merge rules) on validation data. Hysteresis performs about as well as a tuned single threshold; the bigger win is tuning at all, especially per class.

### Cited Findings
**DCASE Task 4 (domestic SED) baselines and tuning**
- The DCASE 2020 Task 4 baseline uses a fixed threshold of 0.5 and a fixed median filter window of 7 frames; the ConformerSED system also uses a fixed 0.5 threshold but a coarse per-class grid search over median filter length — [Giannakopoulos, Pikrakis & Cotronis, "Improving Post-Processing of Audio Event Detectors Using Reinforcement Learning", arXiv:2208.09201 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09201)
- Jointly tuning the threshold and the median window (search space: threshold in (0,1), odd windows 3–21) with an RL agent and an event-based macro-F1 reward raised validation-set F1 from 40.1% to 44.5% (baseline) and from 47.7% to 52.4% (ConformerSED), class-dependent values beating one global pair (43.4% / 51.1%). Thresholding-only tuning was worse than tuning both — [Giannakopoulos et al., arXiv:2208.09201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09201)
- On DCASE data, collar-based F1 on the evaluation set was 51.8% at a fixed threshold of 0.5 vs 57.2% at the threshold that was optimal on the validation set; the authors conclude "threshold-dependent metrics can be biased by the tuning of an operating point" — [Ebbers, Haeb-Umbach & Serizel, "Threshold Independent Evaluation of Sound Event Detection Scores", arXiv:2201.13148 (2022); ICASSP 2022, DOI 10.1109/ICASSP43922.2022.9747556 (venue/DOI not verified this session)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13148)
- The optimal median filter length depends on the operating point: longer filters are better at low false-positive rates (they suppress short FPs), shorter filters are better when targeting high true-positive rates (high recall). Optimal filters are also class-dependent (longer for long events like "Frying", "Running water"). Their sweep used 40 filter lengths from 0 s (none) to 5 s. Post-processing choices changed team rankings in DCASE 2023 Task 4a — [Ebbers, Serizel & Haeb-Umbach, "Post-Processing Independent Evaluation of Sound Event Detection Systems", arXiv:2306.15440 (2023), DCASE 2023 Workshop](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15440)
- Same paper: per-class "best" median filters "are… not the best choice for each individual operating point" — [Ebbers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15440)

**Systematic comparison incl. hysteresis (double threshold)**
- Cances, Guyot & Pellegrini compared post-processing on DCASE 2018 Task 4: moving-average smoothing; statistic-based thresholds (data-wise average, class-independent CIDWA or class-dependent CDDWA); absolute thresholds (CIA/CDA); hysteresis with separate onset/offset thresholds (CIH/CDH); slope-based (CIS/CDS); plus merging segments separated by gaps shorter than a tolerance and removing segments shorter than it. Parameters were optimised by a "dichotomic search" on the test split and checked on the evaluation split — [Cances, Guyot & Pellegrini, "Evaluation of post-processing algorithms for polyphonic sound event detection", arXiv:1906.06909 (2019); WASPAA 2019, DOI 10.1109/WASPAA.2019.8937143 (venue/DOI not verified this session)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06909)
- Results (MIL model, tagging oracle, eval-set event F1): coarse grid search 15.3%, CIDWA 25.8%, CDDWA 29.9%, CIA 37.1%, CIH (hysteresis) 40.7%, CDA 43.9%, CDH 42.9%, CDS 41.0%. Hysteresis beat a single threshold in the class-independent case (40.7 vs 37.1) and was roughly equal in the class-dependent case; the best eval error rate (1.1) came from class-dependent hysteresis — [Cances et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06909)
- Optimal smoothing windows differed per class from 9 frames (Dishes) to 27 (Vacuum cleaner); "Results show the crucial impact of the postprocessing methods on the final detection score"; class-dependent methods gave "greater robustness when switching from the test set to the evaluation set" — [Cances et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06909)
- Note: Cances et al. report F1/error rate, not precision and recall separately, so the recall-specific effect of hysteresis is not isolated there — [Cances et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06909)
- Double-threshold post-processing also appears in "Duration robust sound event detection" (Dinkel & Yu) and its follow-up on weakly supervised SED — [arXiv:1904.03841](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03841v3); [arXiv:2101.07687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07687) (found in search, not read in detail this session)

**DCASE few-shot bioacoustic event detection (Task 5; Nolasco et al. 2023)**
- Task metric: event-based F-measure with IoU >= 30% matching via bipartite graph matching, harmonic mean over datasets — [Nolasco et al., "Learning to detect an animal sound from five examples", arXiv:2305.13210 (2023); Ecological Informatics 77:102258, DOI 10.1016/j.ecoinf.2023.102258 (venue/DOI not verified this session)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13210)
- Baseline post-processing: "predicted events with shorter duration than 60% of the duration of the shortest shot provided for that file are removed" (minimum duration set adaptively from the 5 support examples); template-matching baseline sets a per-file detection threshold from the max cross-correlation value — [Nolasco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13210)
- "Successful systems also commonly used explicit methods to control the duration of the detected events", e.g. post-processing "to delete/merge very short events, or estimating the typical duration from the examples"; the paper cautions that "Because systems are developed based on the development and validation sets alone, the design decisions might not be what works best for the evaluation set." — [Nolasco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13210)
- Surrey DCASE 2022 Task 5 system: minimum segment length 0.4·t̄ (t̄ = mean duration of the labelled shots), merge two segments if combined duration < 0.8·t̄ and the gap is shorter than a minimum, split segments longer than 2·t̄; thresholds varied widely by submission (0.6, 0.95, and an ensemble over 0.5–0.995); "most parameters… are chosen by experience", all reported on the validation set, and the best configuration was withheld because "the model might overfit to the validation dataset" — [Liu et al., "Surrey System for DCASE 2022 Task 5", arXiv:2207.10547 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10547)
- Precision/recall across Surrey submissions: threshold 0.95 + pad/split/merge gave P 55.5% / R 32.1% (F 40.7%); threshold 0.6, no post-processing gave P 76.6% / R 49.5% (F 60.2%); the unsubmitted best config P 76.3% / R 62.6% (F 68.7%). Settings differ at once, so this does not isolate any single post-processing step, but the high-threshold system had by far the lowest recall — [Liu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10547)

### Inferences
- A fixed 0.5 threshold plus a fixed minimum duration is a baseline default, not a tuned optimum. Every paper that tuned these parameters on held-out data found gains (about 4–5 F1 points in Giannakopoulos; 5.4 points from tuning the threshold in Ebbers 2022; much larger in Cances).
- For a recall-first detector, the evidence points to (a) lowering the activation threshold and/or using hysteresis (low offset/continuation threshold, higher onset threshold) and (b) shorter smoothing / min-duration windows, since Ebbers 2023 shows short filters are optimal at high-TPR operating points. A 4 × 0.64 s = 2.56 s minimum duration is a strong low-FP setting. Elephant rumbles are often several seconds long, so this may be acceptable for typical rumbles but will drop short or fragmented ones.
- Making the minimum duration data-driven (as in DCASE Task 5, derived from the duration distribution of the target calls) is standard practice.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that reports precision and recall separately for single-threshold vs hysteresis at matched settings, so a direct "hysteresis raises recall by X" number is unavailable. Cances et al. report only F1/ER.
- The DCASE 2023/2024 Task 4 baseline's exact median filter lengths (per-class in later years) were not read directly from the official baseline repository in this session.

## Q2. Threshold-independent metrics (PSDS, Bilen et al. 2020) and how they handle the activation threshold

### Takeaway
PSDS is the normalised area under a PSD-ROC curve (TP ratio vs effective FP rate per hour) traced by sweeping the system's operating point (normally the activation threshold), up to a maximum FP rate e_max. Later work computes it exactly over all thresholds (sed_scores_eval) and extends it to sweep post-processing too (piPSDS). This only works if the score being swept actually controls which events are created. A rule that fixes the event set at p > 0.5 and then thresholds a mean-probability score is a truncated sweep: the curve cannot extend into the region a lower activation threshold would reach.

### Cited Findings
- PSDS: systems emit detections for operating-point parameters τ_c and "varying τ_c corresponds to moving monotonically across" TP-ratio/FP-rate points. Detections are matched with intersection criteria rather than collars: DTC (detection tolerance, ρ_DTC), GTC (ground-truth intersection, ρ_GTC) and CTTC (cross-trigger, ρ_CTTC), because "inter-labeller disagreements happen mostly at event boundaries" — [Bilen, Ferroni, Tuveri, Azcarreta & Krstulović, "A Framework for the Robust Evaluation of Sound Event Detection", arXiv:1910.08440 (2020); ICASSP 2020, DOI 10.1109/ICASSP40776.2020.9052995 (venue/DOI not verified this session)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08440)
- eFPR adds cross-triggers weighted by α_CT. eTPR is the mean TP ratio across classes minus α_ST × the std across classes. PSDS = normalised area under the PSD-ROC up to e_max, "the maximum eFPR value of interest for the SED application" — [Bilen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08440)
- Bilen et al. note systems differ in where thresholding happens: some "optimize frame classification before factoring frame decisions into event detections", others "form event scores before optimising for event-level thresholds"; the framework is meant to cover any system whose operating point can change, "regardless of their granularity" — [Bilen et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08440)
- Approximating the PSD-ROC with 50 thresholds gave PSDS 0.358 vs the exact 0.400. Ebbers et al. compute exact curves over all thresholds by sorting scores and cumulatively summing per-threshold "deltas" in TP/FP. The same machinery gives collar- and intersection-based PR curves and can select an application operating point. The code is the `sed_scores_eval` package — [Ebbers et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13148)
- piPSDS / miPSDS: PSDS computed over operating points that vary both post-processing (40 median filter lengths) and the decision threshold, taking at each FP rate the post-processing with the highest TPR; it "provides a more complete picture of the overall system behavior and is less biased by hyper parameter tuning" — [Ebbers et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15440)

### Inferences
- For a PR curve or average-precision evaluation of the FruitPunch detector to be meaningful at the high-recall end, the swept quantity should be the frame-level activation threshold, optionally with min-duration and hysteresis as extra sweep dimensions in the piPSDS style. A post-hoc threshold on the mean probability of events already gated at 0.5 is not enough. With p > 0.5 fixed, all cut-offs at or below 0.5 give the same detection set (every event's mean exceeds 0.5), which is the recall cap described in the brief.
- The intersection-based matching (DTC/GTC) in PSDS is more tolerant of boundary errors than collars. This matters for long, fuzzy-onset calls like rumbles.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact DCASE 2021–2024 PSDS1/PSDS2 parameter values (ρ, α, e_max) in this session.

## Q3. Is a fixed 0.5 threshold appropriate for classifiers trained on balanced data but deployed on highly imbalanced audio? (Prior shift, calibration)

### Takeaway
No, not in general. A classifier trained on rebalanced or undersampled data outputs posteriors relative to the *training* prior. Under a deployment prior where events are rare, those outputs are biased upward, and the Bayes-optimal threshold for a given cost ratio is not 0.5. Saerens et al. (2002) and Dal Pozzolo et al. (2015) give closed-form prior corrections. The threshold should be picked from a held-out set that reflects deployment conditions, against the application's precision/recall targets. For a recall-first goal this usually means a threshold well below the default.

### Cited Findings
- Saerens, Latinne & Decaestecker propose a simple EM-based procedure to re-estimate new class priors on unlabelled deployment data and adjust a classifier's posterior outputs accordingly — ["Adjusting the Outputs of a Classifier to New a Priori Probabilities: A Simple Procedure", Neural Computation 14(1):21–41, 2002, DOI 10.1162/089976602753284446 (DOI not verified this session)](https://mlanthology.org/neco/2002/saerens2002neco-adjusting)
- Models trained on undersampled data are not calibrated: "its predicted probabilities will be upwardly biased". The Dal Pozzolo et al. (2015) correction is p̂ = β·p̂_s / (β·p̂_s − p̂_s + 1), with β the majority-class sampling rate. Elkan (2001) and Saerens et al. (2002) are cited as analytical solutions for train/deploy distribution differences, and learning-based calibration (beta calibration, Platt scaling) is suggested as an alternative — [Phelps et al., "Challenges in the calibration of tree-based models for imbalanced classification", arXiv:2412.16209 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16209); original: [Dal Pozzolo, Caelen, Johnson & Bontempi, "Calibrating Probability with Undersampling for Unbalanced Classification", IEEE SSCI 2015](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7376606)
- Empirical SED evidence that 0.5 is suboptimal even without deliberate rebalancing: 51.8% vs 57.2% F1 for 0.5 vs a validation-tuned threshold — [Ebbers et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.13148)

### Inferences
- Worked example of the direction of the effect: if the training set is balanced 1:1 and the deployment frame prior of rumbles is π (e.g. 1%), Saerens-style correction gives p_deploy = π·p / (π·p + (1−π)(1−p)). A model score of 0.5 maps to a deployment posterior of about π. Under the deployment prior, the training-space 0.5 threshold is therefore an extremely conservative cut on a rescaled posterior, and the Bayes threshold for a cost ratio c_FN/c_FP is set by costs, not by 0.5. This is a derivation, not a quoted result.
- Because prior correction is monotonic in p, it does not change the ranking of frames: it changes *which* threshold is appropriate, not which frames can be found. The practical fix is to sweep the activation threshold below 0.5 on a realistic validation set and choose the operating point there, optionally after calibrating (Platt/beta/temperature).
- Temporal aggregation interacts with calibration. Requiring k consecutive frames above threshold makes the event-level false-alarm rate much lower than the frame-level rate. This is another reason a lower frame threshold is tolerable when combined with a minimum duration.

### Gaps
- Guo et al. 2017 ("On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks") and Elkan 2001 ("The Foundations of Cost-Sensitive Learning", IJCAI) are standard references, but I did not retrieve them this session. Their claims (modern NNs are over-confident; optimal threshold p* = c_FP/(c_FP+c_FN) for calibrated posteriors) should be checked before citing.
- I found no bioacoustics paper that explicitly applies Saerens-style prior correction to a detector's frame probabilities.

## Q4. Bioacoustic examples (elephants, whales, birds) of tuning post-processing / thresholds for recall

### Takeaway
Bioacoustic practice routinely moves confidence thresholds away from 0.5 depending on the goal. BirdNET guidance recommends about 0.1–0.3 to maximise detections (recall) and about 0.5 for high-reliability community lists. Elephant rumble detectors use frame thresholding with rising and falling edges and show the trade-off on ROC/PR curves. I found no elephant paper reporting a tuned low threshold or hysteresis rule.

### Cited Findings
- BirdNET (global assessment): "Lower values mean that more potential detections (including some wrong ones) are included in the output." For detecting vocalisations the recommended threshold range is about 0.1–0.3, which "balance[s] recall and precision reasonably well"; about 0.5 suits reliable species lists. Performance is strongly species-specific, and overlap and sensitivity settings interact with the threshold (overlap of about 2 s improved performance; sensitivity 1.5 suggested at low thresholds) — [Pérez-Granados et al., "Optimization of passive acoustic bird surveys: a global assessment of BirdNET settings", Ibis (2025), DOI 10.1111/ibi.70013](https://bou.org.uk/blog-perez-granados-optimizing-birdnet-lessons-from-a-global-initiative/)
- Elephant rumbles: Geldenhuys & Niesler label 100 ms frames as call/no-call with a threshold (θ = 0.5 in their example). A call starts at a rising edge and ends at a falling edge, so endpointing comes directly from the per-frame decisions. They report ROC and PR curves across thresholds, describe over-segmentation of long calls with tails (one call split into several segments, which gap-merging would address), and tune hyperparameters by nested cross-validation — [Geldenhuys & Niesler, "Learning to rumble: Automated elephant call classification, detection and endpointing using deep architectures", arXiv:2410.12082 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12082v1). Caution: the auto-extracted summary stated that "raising the threshold increases sensitivity", which is backwards for a standard probability threshold and probably a summarisation error. Check the original before quoting that sentence.
- DCASE Task 5 bioacoustic systems (birds, mammals, insects) derive min-duration and merge rules from the call durations of the support examples (60% of the shortest shot; 0.4·t̄ / 0.8·t̄) — [Nolasco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13210); [Liu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10547)

### Inferences
- For elephant rumbles (long, low-frequency, often partly masked), the over-segmentation reported by Geldenhuys & Niesler suggests that a "≥4 consecutive frames above 0.5" rule can miss rumbles whose probability dips briefly below 0.5 mid-call. Hysteresis (e.g. onset > t_high, continue while > t_low) or gap merging before applying the minimum duration would directly recover these, and would raise recall.
- The BirdNET guidance is a practitioner precedent: when the goal is to find vocalisations for human review (recall-first), thresholds well below 0.5 are recommended.

### Gaps
- Allen et al. 2021 (humpback whale song CNN, Frontiers in Marine Science, DOI 10.3389/fmars.2021.607321) is a likely example of choosing a threshold for a target precision on long-term PAM data, but the page could not be read this session. Check it before citing specific numbers.
- Bjorck et al. 2019 (forest elephant rumble detection, Elephant Listening Project) and the Cornell ELP detectors were not retrieved, so their threshold and post-processing choices are not documented here.
- I found no bioacoustic study that quantifies recall gained from hysteresis specifically.
