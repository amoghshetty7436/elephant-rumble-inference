Elephant rumble detector · six new designs tested · 3 Nov 2026

Two new designs beat our current detector

We tried six new ways of finding elephant rumbles in 24-hour forest recordings. Two of them are better than the detector we have now: Dasheng (a different "ear" listening to the sound) and Detect, then double-check (a second model that re-checks every alarm). Three were worse, and two didn't finish because of a bug that is now fixed.

How we judge a detector

Fewer false alarms, while still finding the rumbles

A detector listens to a whole day and raises an alarm when it thinks an elephant rumbled. Two things can go wrong: it misses a real rumble, or it raises a false alarm on something else (rain, an engine, a monkey). Someone at ELP has to check every alarm by hand, so false alarms cost people time.

We fix how many rumbles a detector must find, then count its false alarms:

Score = false alarms per hour of recording, while still finding 8 to 8½ rumbles out of every 10. Lower is better. Our current detector scores 0.159, which is about one false alarm every 6 hours.

Each design was trained 5 times from scratch, because results vary a little from run to run. We first compare them on validation days, recordings set aside for choosing. Then each design is tested once on recordings none of them ever saw (Sample A and Sample B from the Nouabalé-Ndoki forest, plus 3 days of vehicle noise).

The six designs

What each one does, and how it did

All of them start the same way. The recording is sped up 32 times so that rumbles, which are too low for most sound models, land in a pitch range a pretrained model knows. That model, the "ear", turns each 0.64 seconds of sound into a list of numbers, a fingerprint. A small classifier then reads the fingerprints and says "rumble" or "not rumble". The designs change one piece of this.

Dasheng: a different ear

Best on validation · 0.129

Swaps the ear. Our current ear (AVES) was trained on animal sounds. Dasheng was trained on 272,000 hours of all kinds of sound. Everything else stays the same.

Result: the fewest false alarms in the forest, and it found the most rumbles in Sample A. But it is fooled more often by vehicles.

Detect, then double-check

Ties on validation · best on tests

Keeps the current detector, then hands each alarm to a second model. That model looks at a 32-second picture of the sound (a spectrogram) and gives its own opinion. It is a standard image network (ResNet-18), trained on the mistakes the first detector made.

Result: on validation it matches the current detector (0.158). On the tests it beats it everywhere, including vehicles.

Day-normalised

Worse · 0.196

Each recorder sounds a bit different. This design subtracts a recording's typical background from every fingerprint, so only what stands out is left.

Result: worse. Removing the background seems to remove useful information too.

BirdAVES: a bird-trained ear

Worse · 0.196

The same ear as now, but trained with many more bird recordings.

Result: worse. More birds did not help with elephants.

Long memory

Clearly worse · 0.286

Lets the classifier read 82 seconds at once instead of 11, using a recurrent network (a BiGRU), the trick that helped with whale calls.

Result: clearly worse. Looking further around a sound doesn't help with rumbles.

Two-stream and Pitch contour

Did not run · bug fixed

Two-stream adds a small picture model looking at the low-pitch spectrogram alongside the ear. Pitch contour tracks the rumble's pitch, which bends up and down, unlike an engine's flat hum.

Both need features computed from the audio, and that step crashed on very short noise clips (rain drops under 2 seconds). This is fixed, and both can run next time the lab PC is free.

What next

Combine the two winners

  1. Dasheng finds candidates, and the double-checker re-checks them. That could keep Dasheng's fewer forest false alarms while the checker removes the vehicle alarms. It takes about 10 hours on the lab PC, because both parts are already computed.
  2. Run Two-stream and Pitch contour, now that the bug is fixed (about 6 hours).
  3. Pick the final design on the validation days only, then test it once.
Words used on this page
Rumble
A very low call forest elephants make, mostly below 30 Hz, too low for people to hear well.
False alarm
The detector says "rumble" but there was none.
Validation days
Recordings kept aside to compare designs and choose settings. Never used for training.
Test sets
Sample A (43 days) and Sample B, hand-checked forest days, plus 3 days of vehicle noise. Used once, at the end.
Ear / encoder
A large model, trained beforehand on lots of sound, that turns audio into fingerprints. We don't retrain it.
Precision
Of all alarms, the share that were real rumbles.
